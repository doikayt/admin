Although we are a secular organization, our core ethos is intimately tied to the Jewish tradition of Tikkun Olam —
the obligation to repair and heal the world — and to the Jewish Labor Bund's commitment to multi-ethnic,
working-class solidarity (more on our founding philosophy at doikayt.org/about.html).

In practice, this shows up directly in our work defending human and civil rights.  Locally we 
work with Vigil4Gaza (youtube.com/@vigil4gaza), regularly
helping with their social media tech. More broadly, we are active members of Tech for Palestine
(techforpalestine.org). On that platform we have met incredibly 
bright young software engineers from conflict zones, and we hope to
bring some of these developers on board for projects as we get funding.

We see this as part of a broader principle: working shoulder-to-shoulder in multi-ethnic coalitions for human,
worker, and free-speech rights is itself a way of eliminating prejudice and discrimination and lessening tension
between communities. Speaking personally, my own experience in these spaces is that my Palestinian Christian and
Muslim comrades are extremely appreciative when Jewish people are hands-on and visible in their events.

With regard to combating community deterioration, we see that deterioration playing out in the declining health,
privacy, and economic security of our fellow Americans. As voting alone becomes an increasingly unreliable avenue
for change, we see community and grassroots organizing as one of the most effective forms of redress — but that
redress depends on organizations having secure digital tools to communicate, coordinate, and mobilize. This is where
our own work fits directly: we build that infrastructure, and we build it specifically to resist the erosion of
those same rights through Big Tech surveillance and deplatforming, so the organizations doing this work retain
control of their own infrastructure rather than depending on platforms that harvest their data and/or silence them
without warning.

Finally, by making our tools available on a completely free, open-source basis, we not only make sophisticated
technology available to grassroots campaigns and organizations that couldn't otherwise afford it — the source code
itself becomes a teaching vehicle, and its modular design allows pieces to be adapted to other community projects.
