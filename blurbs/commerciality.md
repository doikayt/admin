
The specific program we're seeking this Fund to support is the development of (a) GASify
(github.com/doikayt/gas-demodulify-plugin), an open-source framework for Google Apps Script extensions, and (b) its
first application, geoCRM, a lightweight geographic contact-management tool for Google Sheets. A more detailed plan
is available here: github.com/doikayt/fiscal-sponsor-research/blob/master/blurbs/geocrm.md

We will release the core framework and first application as open-source under the MIT license — meaning any future
commercial use, including by us, relies on the same publicly available code under the same open terms as anyone
else; no special IP transfer or privileged access is involved.

So the software will be freely usable by any organization, not just the mission-aligned organizations (who typically
can't afford commercial CRM tooling) that we seek to support. However, we plan to devote a portion of our time to
helping our targeted organizations onboard their staff — that hands-on support, not the licensing terms, is where
our charitable effort is actually targeted.

Development of the packaging and integration software needed to distribute a commercial version of geoCRM to the SMB
market through the Google Workspace Marketplace will be kept arm's-length and separate from the charitable Fund. We
plan to build out that component independently of the grant activity described here, with no cross-subsidization.
That commercial layer will compete with existing for-profit products (e.g., Salesforce Maps, Geopointe) and will be
priced at market rates for that audience, since it isn't the population our charitable purpose is centered on.

If that commercial layer ever generates meaningful revenue, we're considering housing it in a distinct, wholly owned
for-profit subsidiary — keeping it fully separate from the charitable Fund rather than operating it directly. This
isn't a settled structure yet, and we'd very much welcome Tides' guidance on how to set it up correctly, since we'd
rather get this right from the start than have it become an issue later.
