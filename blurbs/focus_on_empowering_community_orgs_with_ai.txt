
Alignment with Tides Mission

Tides' focus on equality and human rights is the closest and clearest match to our work, and it runs deeper than
shared values — it's built into how we design our tools. Organizations doing civil-rights and social-justice work
increasingly depend on commercial Big Tech platforms for core operations, which leaves them vulnerable to sudden
deplatforming — account suspension, service cutoffs, payment processor freezes — often with no warning and
independent of any wrongdoing, simply because the work is politically sensitive. We build open-source, self-hostable
tools specifically to reduce that dependency, so the organizations doing the work retain control of their own
infrastructure rather than operating at the pleasure of a platform that could cut them off. This is also where your
emphasis on powering solutions that come directly from communities historically denied power lines up closely with
our own orientation: technology should be controlled by the people who use it, not by the platforms hosting it.

Quality education is the second strong connection, and it now overlaps directly with environmental responsibility.
We use AI in our own tooling, but treat its environmental cost as a design constraint rather than an afterthought —
favoring smaller, efficient, locally-run models over water sucking, energy-intensive cloud-
scale infrastructure wherever practical.

We're aware that many progressive and grassroots organizations have developed a reflexive skepticism toward AI, and 
the underlying concerns — water and energy consumption chief among them — are legitimate. But we see those costs as 
a consequence of how AI has been built and deployed in a profit-maximizing environment, not as something inherent to
the technology itself. Left entirely in the hands of profit-motivated actors, this 
trajectory leads toward greater economic inequality and more fractured, socially isolated communities — the opposite of what 
organizations like ours and Tides' partners are working toward. We believe there's a better path: technology built 
and used in service of community needs, deliberately mindful of its ecological cost. If organizations like ours cede
this ground entirely to those with no such accountability, the communities we serve lose that fight before it 
starts.

We document our approach on our blog and in training material specifically so other under-resourced organizations
adopting AI tools can do so in a less environmentally damaging way, not just so our own operations are greener. That
makes this simultaneously an education deliverable and a sustainability practice, rather than two separate, thinner
connections. 

The connection to healthy individuals and communities is more indirect but real: the community groups we support are
themselves doing the frontline work of civic participation, mutual aid, and community organizing. Strengthening
their technical capacity and platform independence strengthens their ability to do that work effectively and
durably, which we see as a multiplier on Tides' community-health goals rather than a program we run directly.
