Doikayt — Internal Organizational Profile: For Fiscal Sponsorship & Funding Research

This document describes Doikayt's internal conception of its mission and organizational
orientation and should not necessarily be treated as a complete statement of its public-facing mission.

Status: Early-stage, currently unincorporated
Purpose of this document: Internal working profile for evaluating fiscal sponsors and, 
subsequently, other funding sources. This is not necessarily public-facing language.

1. Organizational Overview

Doikayt is an early-stage nonprofit software collective dedicated to providing
digital products and technical services to organizations and political
campaigns aligned with its mission and values.

The organization is intended to operate as a mission-driven technology
organization rather than a conventional commercial software company. Its
purpose is to use software and technical expertise to strengthen organizations
that may otherwise lack access to sophisticated technology and engineering
resources.

Doikayt intends to fund its development through grants, donations, and eventually
commerical subscriptions and licenses to the software it produces -- 
rather than through conventional venture-capital financing.

The organization is currently unincorporated. It does not want to rush into
incorporation because its optimal permanent legal structure has not yet been
determined.

2. Mission & Organizational Orientation

Doikayt's broader objective is to use digital technologies to strengthen 
the capacity, autonomy, and effectiveness of grassroots and other 
organizations working toward social and political change.

Our values:
    anti-imperialism
    pro-human rights
    pro-tolerance
    pro-free speech
    pro-working-class

3. Activities & Services

Doikayt's principal activity is software development, consulting and systems integration.

Current open source projects we have underway include:

    * Doikayt Wiki — a collaborative, structured knowledge base for curating open source / low cost / ethically produced digital tools useful to mission-aligned organizations.
    * qwiki — a CLI framework for deploying MediaWiki content maintained as local Markdown.
    * gas-demodulify-plugin — tooling for building complex Google Apps Script applications with modern TypeScript/Webpack workflows.
    * build-tools — automated documentation-generation and verification tooling designed to detect documentation drift during builds.
    * typescript-build-config — shared TypeScript development, testing, formatting, and release infrastructure.

Note that the latter three projects form the basis of a software stack for development of 
Google Application Script (GAS)-based apps such as the targeting tool we built for the Butch Ware
Campaign. The goal of that project was to filter volunteer database entries by geographical proximity to a 
particular event, and we provided this functionality via a GAS extension hook to Google spread sheets. 



We develop our software via an AI-native, spec-to-code pipeline.

5. Political & Campaign Activity

This is a material internal consideration for fiscal-sponsor selection.

Doikayt intends to be able to provide legitimate technical services to political candidates and campaigns, including activities such as:

technology selection and evaluation;
software configuration;
customization;
implementation;
technical infrastructure;
software development;
related technical assistance.

The ability to perform such work is considered an important organizational requirement.

Doikayt should therefore not be placed into a structure whose legal or operational restrictions would prevent it from carrying out its intended campaign-related activities.

A sponsor's mission alignment is insufficient if its legal structure or sponsorship requirements make these activities impossible.

STRATEGY UPDATE (2026-08-12): Doikayt is deferring campaign-services work rather than
restructuring the organization around it. Estimated organic likelihood of landing an
opportunistic campaign engagement in the near term: ~20%. Near-term fiscal-sponsor
search is now focused on housing Doikayt's generic, publicly-available open-source
software work under a straightforward c3 relationship, without changes to public
branding/messaging. A c4 relationship (for campaign-services work) is parked, to be
revisited once campaign engagements actually materialize in the pipeline. See
sponsors.json change_log entries dated 2026-08-12 for the re-screening this triggered.

6. Funding & Revenue Model

Doikayt is pursuing a bootstrap-first strategy rather than conventional venture-capital financing.

Potential funding sources include:

grants;
philanthropic support;
contributions;
earned revenue;
fees for software and technical services;
potentially other mission-compatible revenue sources.

Doikayt is not primarily motivated by providing donors with charitable tax deductions.

The organization is interested in determining a sustainable model combining mission-oriented funding with, where appropriate, earned revenue.

The eventual balance between grants, contributions, and earned revenue remains undetermined.

7. Current Legal / Structural Position

Doikayt is currently unincorporated.

It has not yet determined the optimal permanent legal structure.

A 501(c)(4) structure was previously under serious consideration because Doikayt intends
to retain the ability to work with political campaigns. Per the strategy update in Section 5,
this is now a longer-term consideration rather than a near-term structural requirement.

Other structural possibilities—including relationships between nonprofit and for-profit entities—may ultimately warrant consideration.

Doikayt deliberately does not want to incorporate prematurely simply to establish a formal nonprofit entity.

The organization wants to use its early operating and funding experience to inform the eventual legal structure.

Current strategic hypothesis

Fiscal sponsorship may provide an appropriate intermediate organizational structure while Doikayt determines its permanent structure.

This is a hypothesis to be tested, not an established conclusion.

8. Fiscal Sponsorship Requirements

For the initial research phase, fiscal sponsorship is the preferred path to investigate before incorporation.

A potentially suitable sponsor should ideally allow Doikayt to:

remain initially unincorporated;
operate under the Doikayt identity;
receive grants and other permissible funding;
conduct its intended software activities;
receive and manage earned revenue where permissible;
hire employees or contractors if necessary;
develop and own/control its intellectual property to the extent legally possible;
provide legitimate technical services to political campaigns (deferred near-term per Section 5);
retain substantial operational autonomy;
eventually separate from the sponsor and establish an independent entity.

Hard constraint (near-term, revised)
A sponsor should be considered structurally incompatible if its sponsorship arrangement would
prevent Doikayt from conducting its generic, publicly-available open-source software work,
receiving grants for that work, or maintaining reasonable operational autonomy. Since campaign-
related technical activity is now deferred rather than an active line of business, a sponsor's
inability to host that specific activity is no longer, by itself, disqualifying — see
sponsors.json for the live screening logic. However, an important open question for any
Model A (comprehensive) sponsor is what happens if an opportunistic campaign-services
engagement arises while sponsored (see standard outreach question in sponsors.json).

9. Long-Term Organizational Design

Doikayt is interested in experimenting with DAO / digitally autonomous organization principles as a potential long-term organizational model.

This is an organizational-design objective, not a current fiscal-sponsorship requirement.

The underlying objectives include exploring whether technology can support:

transparent operational rules;
transparent and auditable financial activity;
clearly defined governance procedures;
distributed organizational decision-making;
reduced dependence on opaque discretionary authority;
automated or programmatically enforced organizational processes where appropriate.

Blockchain, smart contracts, and DAO mechanisms are potential tools for investigating these objectives, rather than ends in themselves.

Doikayt intends to determine empirically whether such mechanisms actually advance its organizational objectives before adopting them.

A fiscal sponsor does not need to support DAO governance or blockchain-based financial control for Doikayt to be considered a viable sponsored project.

This characteristic may nevertheless be relevant when assessing mission affinity with prospective sponsors, particularly organizations concerned with digital rights, technological self-determination, open technology, transparency, or decentralized governance.

10. Organizational Philosophy

Doikayt's organizational philosophy is oriented toward:

bottom-up rather than top-down organization;
transparency rather than opacity;
organizational autonomy;
technological independence;
member/participant agency;
explicit understandable, and collaboratively developed rules;
practical use of technology to distribute organizational capacity.

These principles are aspirational and evolutionary rather than a fixed governance constitution.

11. Important Distinctions

The following distinctions should be preserved throughout the fiscal-sponsorship research:

Mission ≠ political campaign

Doikayt may serve political campaigns without being fundamentally a political campaign organization.

Software company ≠ conventional commercial software company

Software development is the mechanism through which Doikayt advances its broader mission.

Nonprofit ≠ necessarily 501(c)(3)

Doikayt is currently considering a 501(c)(4) structure and does not assume that 501(c)(3) is appropriate. (See Section 5/7 strategy update: c4 is now a longer-term consideration.)

Fiscal sponsorship ≠ permanent legal structure

Fiscal sponsorship is currently being investigated as an intermediate structure while the optimal permanent structure remains undetermined.

DAO interest ≠ current DAO requirement

DAO principles are a long-term organizational-design experiment, not a prerequisite for operating now.

12. Open Questions

The following remain deliberately unresolved:

Is fiscal sponsorship the optimal initial structure?
What type of fiscal sponsor is best suited to Doikayt?
Is a 501(c)(4) ultimately the optimal independent structure?
How should earned revenue be structured within the eventual organization?
Would a nonprofit/for-profit subsidiary structure eventually make sense?
What activities can appropriately be conducted through a fiscal sponsor?
How should intellectual property be owned and licensed?
How much operational autonomy can be retained under fiscal sponsorship?
What governance model should Doikayt ultimately adopt?
Which DAO/decentralization mechanisms actually provide meaningful organizational benefits?
[Added 2026-08-12] For a Model A (comprehensive) sponsor, what happens if an opportunistic
campaign-services engagement arises while sponsored — is it declinable, referable, or does
it necessarily become the sponsor's own compliance concern?

These questions should be treated as research questions rather than assumptions.
